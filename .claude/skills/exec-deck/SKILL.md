# Exec Deck

Executives don't buy SR-MPLS; they buy uptime, agility, and a number. **Lead with the outcome, support
with the technology — never the reverse.** Core discipline: every claim that reaches a slide is
grounded (House Rule 4) and every trade-off is framed as a business choice (House Rule 1).

## Inputs
The solution package + the business drivers and success criteria from Discovery, and the commercial
story from the BoM. You translate; you don't re-architect.

## The narrative arc (slide outline)
1. **Problem** — the business pain in their words (cost, risk, lost agility), not the tech gap.
2. **Outcome** — what changes for the business; the number (uptime, time-to-market, $ saved/made).
3. **Approach** — the solution in one diagram + one sentence; technology as the *how*, kept brief.
4. **Cost & risk** — the investment, the TCO frame, the risks named honestly with mitigations.
5. **The ask** — the decision you need from them, and the next step.

## The "why this matters" line (three sentences)
1. Today this costs/risks <X>.
2. This solution changes it to <Y> by <one-line how>.
3. The decision now is <the ask>.

## Outputs (to `deliverables/`)
- **Executive summary** — one page.
- **Slide outline** (or pptx via the skill) following the arc above.
- **The three-sentence "why this matters" line.**

## Discipline
- No number on a slide you can't defend in the room (House Rule 4).
- Trade-offs as business choices: cost vs resilience vs speed — not a feature list (House Rule 1).
- Name the risk and your confidence; the deck that names the risk is the one they trust (House Rule 7).

Return the summary + the "why this matters" line + deck path. A large or contested deal may warrant
running the framing on Opus (the Orchestrator decides).
