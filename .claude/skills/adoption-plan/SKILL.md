# Adoption Plan

Delivered is not adopted. A working network that nobody operates well, or whose capabilities go unused,
hasn't delivered its value. Core discipline: **every barrier has an owner and a date — a plan without
owners is a wish** — and **metrics trace to the original business drivers, not vanity numbers**
(House Rule 1).

## Inputs
The business drivers and success criteria from the Discovery brief (what value was promised), and the
delivered solution (what capabilities now exist). Pull the customer's org/skills context from memory.

## Procedure
1. **Find the barriers** across four dimensions:
   - **Skills** — does the team know how to operate/extend it?
   - **Process** — do change/ops processes use the new capability, or route around it?
   - **Tooling** — are dashboards/automation in the operators' hands and workflows?
   - **Organizational** — ownership, incentives, competing priorities.
2. **Barrier → action → owner → milestone.** Each barrier gets a concrete action, a **named owner**,
   and a **milestone date**. No orphan barriers.
3. **Value scorecard** — define the metrics that prove the outcome, each traced to a Discovery business
   driver. Baseline now, target + date.
4. **Enablement sessions** — ATX / Accelerator agendas that move the team up the adoption curve
   (awareness → capability → habit), mapped to the barriers they remove.

## Outputs (to `deliverables/`)
- **Adoption plan** — the `barrier → action → owner → milestone` table.
- **Session agendas** — ATX/Accelerator outlines.
- **Value scorecard** — `metric → business driver → baseline → target → date`.

## Discipline
- Owners are **people/roles**, not "the team". Dates are real.
- Vanity metrics (logins, tickets closed) only count if they trace to a business driver.
- Be honest where adoption depends on a customer decision you don't control (House Rule 7) — flag it.

Return a summary + plan path.
